## Illustrative, not claims

The trail is a metaphor and is never presented as footage or as measured behaviour:

- the walker, the route behind him with its unlabelled ticks and the forks he did not take (no
  years are shown: the article gives none);
- the pile of sheets and the glowing one at the bottom; the sheet unfolding into a stretch;
- the small light running up the steep stretch, and the tiles it lights (the stack of files from
  the first scene, as a mountain). The walker stops where it starts: the article's "the model
  outran him at the end";
- the walker's easel, with only the first part of the stretch drawn ("cannot yet explain the last
  stretch");
- the second figure with a thick manuscript, arriving on a path of its own that does not go to
  the summit (the article: "The status is not comparable");
- the crowd, and the map that climbs towards the light and stops short of it (the map's height
  is not a measure of anything);
- the new forks with people on them at the end ("far more routes can now be explored");
- the number of tiles in the stack (280) and in the mountain: decoration, not a count.
